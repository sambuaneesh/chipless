How ties are broken

The kicker

When two players have the same pair, two pair, three of a kind or four of a kind, the next-highest card in their five-card hand (the kicker) decides. With a board of K♥ 7♦ 7♣ 2♠ 9♥, a player holding K♠ Q♦ and one holding K♦ J♣ both have two pair, kings and sevens. The queen beats the jack, so K-Q wins.

Only five cards count

Your hand is always exactly five cards. If the board gives both players the same best five cards, their sixth and seventh cards don't matter and the pot is split.

The board plays

Sometimes the best hand is the five cards on the table, for example a straight on the board that nobody can beat. Everyone still in the hand then ties and splits the pot.

Suits never break ties

In Texas Hold'em no suit is higher than another. Two flushes are compared card by card from the top; if every card matches in rank, it's a split.

Common mix-ups

  • Flush beats straight. Five cards of one suit are harder to make than five in a row.
  • Full house beats flush. Three of a kind plus a pair outranks any flush.
  • The ace plays high or low in a straight. A-2-3-4-5 (the "wheel") is the lowest straight; 10-J-Q-K-A is the highest. Straights can't wrap around (Q-K-A-2-3 is not a straight).
  • Two pair is compared top pair first. Kings and twos beats queens and jacks.
  • A full house is ranked by its three of a kind. Kings full of sevens beats sevens full of kings.

Questions

Does a flush beat a straight?

Yes. A flush ranks above a straight, and a full house ranks above both.

What happens when two players have the same hand?

The pot is split equally between them. If it can't be divided evenly, the odd chip goes to the first tied player to the left of the dealer button.

Do I have to use both of my cards?

No. You can use both, one or neither of your hole cards. Whatever five of the seven cards make the best hand is your hand.
